## What This Is

A portfolio that documents how I think about product problems: framing them with evidence, diagnosing root causes, prioritizing under constraints, and sequencing solutions that ship.

Five case studies covering:
- **Coinbase**: Transparency gap (why speed without visibility doesn't fix trust)
- **Replit**: Pricing predictability (why users churned over unknowable costs, not high costs)
- **WHOOP**: Trust architecture (three failures in three years = structural problem)
- **LinkedIn Jobs**: Cold-start matching (early-career users churn before the graph has data)
- **Notion AI**: Delivery foresight (reasoning over content Notion already owns)

Plus one built product:
- **FeedbackLens**: NLP-powered tool that groups 500+ feedback pieces into themes, automating synthesis PMs usually do manually

Each case study shows the work: evidence citations, RICE scoring, explicit trade-offs, what got cut and why.

## Why a README

You'd open the portfolio to see the case studies. You're here because you want to know:

### 1. **Is this real or theoretical?**
Mixed. Coinbase, Replit, WHOOP, and LinkedIn are independent case studies built from public evidence (CFPB complaints, lawsuits, user stories, market data). Notion AI is speculative (full PRD for a product that doesn't exist yet). FeedbackLens is a product I built, shipped, and documented with PRD + technical decisions.

I flag the difference throughout—no primary interviews is noted as a limitation in LinkedIn, competitive analysis uses public data in Replit, Notion is explicitly labeled speculative.

### 2. **What's the technical stack?**
Next.js 16, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, Vercel. Static generation, WCAG AA+ accessibility, editorial design system.

Built the portfolio itself as a product decision: depth over breadth, content-first, magazine aesthetic over playful. Design principles match what I'd pitch in a PRD.

### 3. **What makes this different from typical PM portfolios?**
Most PM portfolios show shipped features with metrics. This one shows the reasoning process:
- Not "here's what I shipped" → "here's the problem, here's why this solution earned the right to ship"
- Not "I used RICE" → Here's the scoring table, here's why the flashy feature ranked lowest
- Not "users wanted X" → Here's the CFPB data, here's the lawsuit, here's the user story verbatim

### 4. **Why the technical background matters**
I spent two years building software before transitioning to product. That shows up as:
- Understanding constraints (Replit's architecture genuinely makes estimation harder, Coinbase's compliance is non-negotiable)
- Knowing when AI earns its place (reasoning scaffolds, trust before autonomy)
- Speaking both PRD and engineering constraint language
